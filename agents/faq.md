## How this file works

- **Inside this file, the Operator answers.** It replies directly, in your
  voice, using the answer written here.
- **Outside this file, the Operator escalates.** It drafts a reply and flags it
  for you with one line on why.
- **Every time it gets one wrong, add a line here** rather than correcting the
  same thing twice. This file is the only place the Operator learns.

Write answers the way you would actually send them — full sentences, your
tone, no placeholders left inside the answer text. The Operator sends what is
written here verbatim enough that a `[FILL IN]` left in the body will go out
looking like one. `agents/voice.md` is the standard the wording follows.

A `> Source:` line is not part of the answer. The validator stops reading at
it, so it never reaches a client. It exists so you can check any sentence
against the page it came from before you trust it.

## Escalate always — never answer from this file

These never get an automatic reply, even if something below looks close:
refunds, complaints, legal questions, price or scope negotiation, timeline
commitments, anything involving a payment that failed, anything from a client
who is unhappy. Draft and flag.

The rates below are a published band, not a quote. Any specific number for a
specific job is a negotiation, and negotiations escalate.

## Process and timeline

**What happens after I sign?**
The deposit lands and the work starts the same day. First I take control of the
domain, hosting and email, because that is the piece that holds everything else
up if it waits. The site build runs alongside it over the next two weeks. Your
Google Business Profile goes up on day one and the directory listings follow
inside the first three days. Every file I produce goes into a Google Drive
folder for your firm, which is yours to keep.

> Source: Robert Davis P.A. Master Plan, session log 2026-05-15 — deposit
> received, engagement active, digital footprint audit, citation playbook,
> Drive folder created, all on the day the deposit landed. Block targets in
> both master plans: domain and Google Business Profile day 1, directories
> days 1-3, site build days 1-14.

**How long does a typical engagement take?**
Two weeks from deposit to live is the target for a standard build, and the
fastest so far was six days. What actually sets the date is how quickly the
domain access, the headshot and the photos come back to me. The build is rarely
the slow part. Content and blog work after launch runs on a longer clock,
roughly six months in three stages.

> Source: Robert Davis P.A. — deposit 2026-05-15, site live 2026-05-21, six
> days. Site build target days 1-14 in both master plans. Six-month figure from
> the client-facing "Gerlach Law — Website Phase 2 & Content Strategy"
> (2026-06-20): Phase 2A month 1, 2B months 2-3, 2C months 4-6. The
> client-side caveat is written because the one engagement that ran long was
> blocked seven weeks on a DNS handover, not on the build.

**How do we communicate during the work?**
Email is the main channel, and anything that matters gets written down there so
we both have it. Text works for quick things. If something is stuck for more
than a week, we book a short screen share and clear it live rather than trading
messages about it.

> Source: practice across both engagements — client edits arrive by email as
> Google Docs, chases go out by text, calls happen ad hoc. The seven-day rule
> is DCF's own `working-rules.md`: "when work is blocked on a client, note the
> date and follow up within 7 days." The screen-share escalation is the
> correction adopted 2026-06-23: "any client blocker older than 7 days gets a
> booked screen-share, not another text."

**What do you need from me to get started?**
Four things, and nothing goes live without them. Access to your domain
registrar, so the domain can point at the new site. A card on file for hosting
and email, both of which stay registered to you. Your logo file and a real
headshot of you, not a stock photo. And a decision on which booking tool you
want, so the contact form has somewhere to send people. Two more can run alongside the
build: photos of your office for the Google profile, and three to five past
clients willing to leave a review.

> Source: Gerlach Master Plan, "Hard blockers (nothing goes live without
> these)" — registrar access, headshot confirmation, card on file, booking tool
> confirmation — plus "can run in parallel": office photos, testimonials. Logo
> file from the client-facing follow-up draft on the same page.
